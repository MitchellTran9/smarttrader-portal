## Technical Breakdown: Why Do Hackers Steal Cookies Instead of Passwords?

In the past, malware relied on keyloggers to record every keystroke in order to capture passwords. Today, the strategy has completely shifted. Modern botnets attack browser temporary memory and profile storage directories directly (whether Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or anti-detect browsers).

### The Working Mechanism of Session-Stealing Trojans
When you log into ChatGPT, Claude, Facebook advertising accounts, or crypto exchanges, the system issues the browser a time-bound **Session Cookie** (for instance, maintaining login status for 30 days).
- Malware sneaks onto the computer via cracked software, shared MMO tools downloaded from the internet, or archive files containing malicious scripts.
- They silently compress the entire `Local Storage` and `Cookies` directories and transmit them back to the hacker's C&C (Command and Control) server.
- The malicious actors simply inject these cookies into their own browsers, instantly transforming into "you" **without ever triggering an OTP or 2FA prompt**.
